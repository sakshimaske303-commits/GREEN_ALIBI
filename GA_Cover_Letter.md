Dear Editor,

I am submitting my manuscript titled "Testing the Fluorescence Advantage: Does Solar-Induced Fluorescence Signal Agricultural Drought Earlier than NDVI in Marathwada, India?" for consideration as a Research Article in the International Journal of Applied Earth Observation and Geoinformation.

The study tests a common expectation: that Solar-Induced Fluorescence (SIF), being tied to photosynthesis, should register agricultural drought earlier than NDVI, one of the signals India's drought-management process and PMFBY crop insurance rely on. I compared cropland-weighted GOSIF v2 SIF with MODIS NDVI, timed to each pixel's observation date, across the eight districts of Marathwada over nine Kharif seasons (2015–2023), and I kept two questions apart that are often run together: whether SIF's seasonal decline starts before NDVI's, and whether SIF departs from its normal-year range before NDVI does in a drought year.

The main answer is negative. When each index is compared with its own normal-year behaviour, SIF is not an earlier drought alarm than NDVI: in the two rainfall-defined drought years (2015, 2018) and in most drought district-years, the two indices raised the alarm together or NDVI came first. SIF's normal seasonal decline appears to start one to five weeks before NDVI's when each curve is scaled over the whole season, but I show that this lead comes mostly from NDVI not returning to its pre-season level after the monsoon; scaled over each series' own decline, the lead is small, absent in three years, and no longer differs between districts. MODIS NIRv and EVI from the same composites lead NDVI by as much as SIF does, and SIF does not lead NIRv, so the remaining lead is a property of NDVI rather than of fluorescence. For the policy question, I benchmark satellite alarms against an approximation of the rainfall-based Trigger-1 from India's Manual for Drought Management and against the official 2015 and 2018 declarations.

I believe the paper fits the journal's scope because it applies earth observation data to a concrete agricultural and policy problem, reports a negative result on the drought-alarm question, and identifies a normalisation artefact that can inflate SIF–NDVI phenology lags. The checks include an independent SIF product, two normalisations plus cross-correlation, block-bootstrap uncertainty, district-level tests, spatial autocorrelation, and a crop-yield check against official district statistics. The full pipeline and data are openly available. Only two drought years are available in the study period, and I state this and the other limitations plainly in the manuscript.

This manuscript is original and has not been published in, or submitted to, any other journal. I am the sole author and have approved this submission.

Thank you for considering my manuscript. I look forward to your response.
